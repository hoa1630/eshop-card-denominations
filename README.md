## The terms that decide whether a code is usable

Nintendo's eShop card terms are short and unusually blunt. These six points are the ones that change purchasing decisions.

**1. U.S. accounts only.** The terms say "Valid only with U.S. Accounts." This is a validity condition, not a recommendation. A US card applied to an account set to another country will fail, and there is no conversion path. Some resale channels go further and state the buyer must physically reside in the US; Nintendo's own wording only references the account country. Either way, confirm your Nintendo Account country before paying.

**2. The balance attaches to one account and never moves.** Once you enter the code, "the code balance will be associated with your Account and will be non-transferable. There will be no remaining balance on the code." The terms add that the balance "may only be used on a single Nintendo eShop account." Buy for the wrong profile and the money is stranded there.

**3. $800 is a hard ceiling.** The card terms mention a maximum unused account balance without giving the number. The support documentation does: your total balance cannot exceed $800 USD (or $1,100 CAD / $5,000 MXN in those regions). If a redemption fails for no obvious reason, this is usually why. Spend some balance, then redeem.

**4. Lost or stolen codes are not replaced, and the balance is non-refundable.** The terms state the code "will not be replaced by Nintendo or retailer if the code is lost or stolen," and that the balance "cannot be redeemed for cash and is non-refundable unless required by law." For a physical card this covers a lost receipt and a scratched-off panel alike. For a code bought from a reseller, it means Nintendo will not arbitrate your dispute.

**5. No expiry, no fees, not reloadable.** "Code balance does not expire, and there are no fees associated with the code. This card is non-reloadable." You can sit on an unredeemed code for years. Once redeemed, the balance sits in the account indefinitely.

**6. A physical card is worthless until the register activates it.** The first line of the terms is "No value until activated at register." If you're buying plastic off a rack, make sure the cashier actually scans it.

## Is a card that sells below face value worth buying?

Sometimes. The discount is compensation for giving up Nintendo's protections, so the question is whether you're getting paid enough for that risk.

Buy the discounted card when all three of these are true:

- You're buying for your own account, already signed in and ready to redeem.
- You'll enter the code within minutes of receiving it, not next month.
- You accept that if the code fails, your only recourse is the seller, not Nintendo.

Pay full face value at a retailer instead when any of these apply:

- It's a gift. Handing someone a code that might not redeem is a bad trade for $2 of savings.
- You're stockpiling for a sale that hasn't happened yet. Codes don't expire, but sellers and account situations do.
- You're buying $100 or more. At that size, the percentage discount matters less than the certainty.

## How to redeem a 16-character code

1. From the HOME menu, launch **Nintendo eShop**.
2. Select the account you want the funds on. This choice is permanent.
3. Select **Enter Code** on the left side of the screen.
4. Type the 16-character code from the card and select **OK**. Nintendo notes that letters and symbols not used in these codes are disabled on the on-screen keyboard, which cuts down on typos.
5. Select **Confirm**, then **Add**.

The funds appear in your balance immediately and can be spent in the same session. If the Add step fails, check your balance against the $800 cap before assuming the code is bad.

## If you'd rather not buy a card at all

Two other funding methods exist and both hit the same ceiling.

A credit card must have been issued in the same country as the eShop you're using — US-issued cards work in the US eShop and nowhere else. PayPal funding is limited to account holders in the US, Canada and Mexico aged 18 or older. Both are subject to the same $800 maximum balance.

A card still makes sense as a gift, as a way to budget, or for someone without a US-issued credit card.

## FAQ

**Does the card work on Switch 2?**
Yes. Nintendo's gift card page states that content is delivered to your Nintendo Switch or Nintendo Switch 2 system, and the catalogue is described as more than 10,000 new, classic and indie games.

**Does the balance expire?**
No. The terms state the code balance does not expire and carries no fees.

**Can I get a refund?**
No. The balance cannot be redeemed for cash and is non-refundable unless law requires otherwise.

**I bought a US card but my account is set to another country. Can I fix it?**
Not by moving the balance. The code is valid only with US accounts, and a redeemed balance cannot be transferred between accounts. Confirm your account country in System Settings before you buy.

**Can I stack multiple cards?**
Yes, up to the $800 total balance limit. Two $50 cards redeem fine on a fresh account; a fifth one on an account already holding $750 will not.

**Do I need anything else to use the funds?**
Internet access and acceptance of the Nintendo Account User Agreement. Some downloaded content needs separate accessories or games.

## What to buy, by situation

- **One indie game or a DLC pack:** the $10 or $20 card. The $20 gives you room for a sale price.
- **A full-price Switch 2 release:** the $50 card, or two if you're also covering DLC.
- **A gift for someone whose account balance you can't see:** face value at a mainstream retailer. You avoid both the resale risk and the awkward conversation about a code that didn't work.
- **A household that buys several games a year:** the $100 card at face value, as long as the balance stays well under $800.

Two checks before you pay. First, confirm the Nintendo Account country is set to the United States — this is the one mistake with no recovery. Second, look at your current eShop balance and subtract it from $800; that's your real headroom, and it may be smaller than the card you're about to buy.
